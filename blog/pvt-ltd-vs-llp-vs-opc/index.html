Private Limited vs LLP vs OPC: Which Business Structure is Right for You?

Sakthi Consultancy Team May 2026 6 min read

One of the most common questions we get from first-time entrepreneurs across Hyderabad, Vijayawada, and across India: "Should I register a Private Limited Company, an LLP, or an OPC?"

The honest answer is: it depends on your situation. But after helping 500+ founders choose their structure, we've developed a clear framework. This guide will give you a straight answer — no jargon, no filler.

The Quick Answer

Choose Private Limited if:

  • You have 2 or more co-founders
  • You plan to raise funding (angel, VC, bank loans)
  • You want to build a scalable, investor-ready business
  • You're building a tech startup, e-commerce, or product company

Choose LLP if:

  • You are a CA, lawyer, doctor, architect, or consultant
  • You have 2+ partners but do not need investor funding
  • You want lower annual compliance costs
  • Profit distribution flexibility is important to you

Choose OPC if:

  • You are a solo founder with no co-founders
  • You do not plan to raise equity investment
  • You are a freelancer or independent consultant wanting corporate credibility
  • Your projected turnover is under ₹2 crore

Private Limited Company — Who it's for

A Pvt Ltd is the gold standard for serious businesses in India. It's what banks, investors, and enterprise clients recognise. It can have up to 200 shareholders and issue different classes of shares — which is why it's the only structure that supports venture capital and angel investment.

The trade-off: Higher annual compliance. A Pvt Ltd must file AOC-4, MGT-7, conduct board meetings, maintain minute books, and get a mandatory audit every year. These compliance costs are typically ₹15,000–₹50,000/year if handled by a professional firm.

If you're building a business you intend to scale, hire employees, and potentially raise funding for — Pvt Ltd is the right answer. Always.

LLP — Who it's for

The LLP is ideal for professional service firms: law firms, CA firms, architect practices, consulting partnerships. It has limited liability like a company, but operates and is taxed more like a partnership firm.

Key advantages over Pvt Ltd:

  • Only 2 annual filings (Form 8 and Form 11) vs 4+ for Pvt Ltd
  • No mandatory audit below ₹40 lakh turnover or ₹25 lakh contribution
  • No Dividend Distribution Tax — profit distributions are more tax-efficient
  • Flexible profit-sharing arrangements between partners

The trade-off: An LLP cannot issue equity shares. If you ever want angel or VC funding, you'll need to convert to a Pvt Ltd — which is possible but adds cost and complexity. Also, LLPs are not eligible for DPIIT Startup India recognition if incorporated as an LLP after certain conditions.

If you're a CA practice or law firm in Hyderabad: LLP is almost always the right answer. The compliance savings alone make it worth it.

One Person Company (OPC) — Who it's for

The OPC was created specifically for solo entrepreneurs who want corporate protection without needing a partner. It's governed under the Companies Act 2013, like a Pvt Ltd, which means it has the same credibility and legal standing.

Key advantages:

  • Full ownership and control — no partners, no board dilution
  • Limited liability — personal assets protected
  • Higher credibility than sole proprietorship with banks and clients
  • Easy to convert to Private Limited when your business grows

The trade-offs:

  • Cannot raise equity funding from investors
  • Must appoint a nominee (an Indian citizen who takes over if the sole director is incapacitated)
  • Must convert to Pvt Ltd if paid-up capital exceeds ₹50 lakh or turnover exceeds ₹2 crore
  • Not eligible for DPIIT Startup India recognition

The "Not Sure" Scenario

In our experience, most founders fall into one of these patterns:

  • "I have a co-founder and a startup idea" → Private Limited, always
  • "I'm a freelancer / consultant going solo" → OPC (upgrade to Pvt Ltd when you need a partner or investor)
  • "I'm a CA/lawyer starting a practice with a partner" → LLP
  • "I'm opening a physical retail shop / restaurant" → Proprietorship or OPC; Pvt Ltd if you plan multiple outlets
  • "I'm not sure yet if I'll raise funding" → Pvt Ltd. It's easier to not raise funding in a Pvt Ltd than to convert an LLP or OPC later.
